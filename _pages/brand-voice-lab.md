As AI systems and AI influencers increasingly speak *for* and *about* brands, a brand
no longer has one voice — it has (at least) three. This lab tracks the gap between a
brand's **official voice** (what the brand says), its **consumers' voice** (what
customers say back), and the **AI-generated voice** (what large language models say
when asked). Where these diverge — Tesla is the starkest case below — is where brand
strategy, consumer research, and AI intersect.

Data & Methods
-----

Data compiled July 2026. Each voice draws on a different kind of source:

- **Official voice** — hand-collected from brand newsrooms, press releases, SEC filings,
  and trade-press coverage of flagship campaigns (2024–2026). Every quote links to its source.
- **Consumer voice** — public satisfaction and reputation indices: the
  [American Customer Satisfaction Index](https://theacsi.org/) (2025 studies), the
  [Axios Harris Poll 100](https://www.axios.com/2025/05/20/axios-harris-poll-company-reputation-ranking)
  reputation rankings, and [YouGov BrandIndex](https://business.yougov.com/) — plus documented
  consumer reactions from news coverage and review platforms (which skew toward complainants,
  and are labeled as such).
- **AI-generated voice** — authentic LLM outputs: each brand summary and influencer-style post
  was generated by Claude (Anthropic) on the date shown, using fixed prompts ("describe the
  brand to a consumer" / "write a lifestyle-influencer post"). Nothing in this column is
  paraphrased — it is the model's actual voice. In addition, the model rated every brand on a
  fixed five-attribute rubric — **Warmth, Quality, Innovation, Value, Trust** (0–10) — shown
  as bars in the AI column, following the structured LLM-elicitation paradigm of
  [Li, Castelo, Katona & Sarvary (2024)](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mksc.2023.0454).
  Current limitation, stated plainly: one model, one run — a multi-run, multi-model replication
  with sampling variance is the planned next step.

Each brand also carries a **voice-valence strip**: an ordinal −2…+2 coding of each voice's
valence toward the brand. The official voice is anchored at +2 by construction (brands speak
well of themselves — the anchor makes the *gap* interpretable), the consumer coding follows
the cited satisfaction/reputation indices, and the AI coding follows the valence of the
model's own summary. The official-minus-consumer spread flags where brand self-presentation
and consumer reality diverge most (Tesla being the current extreme). This coding is done by
the curator and disclosed as such — it is a reading aid, not a measurement instrument.

The design follows current practice in the marketing literature. Eliciting brand perceptions
directly from LLMs is a validated measurement approach — LLM-generated brand ratings agree
with human survey data at rates above 75% ([Li, Castelo, Katona & Sarvary 2024, *Marketing
Science*](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mksc.2023.0454)) — though with known
caveats about preference alignment ([Goli & Singh 2024, *Marketing
Science*](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mksc.2023.0306)) and the causal validity
of LLM-simulated experiments ([Gui & Toubia 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.15524)).
Scaling the consumer column beyond indices to raw consumer content would combine two
modalities. For text, transformer-based sentiment models — the field standard is SiEBERT
([Hartmann, Heitmann, Siebert & Schamp 2023, *IJRM*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167811622000477)) —
or zero-shot LLM sentiment classification, which now matches fine-tuned models
([Krugmann & Hartmann 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40547-024-00143-4)),
with attribute-level extensions ([Chakraborty, Kim & Sudhir 2022, *JMR*](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/00222437211052500)).
For images — where much of the consumer voice now lives — the "visual listening in"
approach mines consumer-posted photos with deep learning (BrandImageNet) to measure the
brand attributes consumers actually portray, validated against perception surveys
([Liu, Dzyabura & Mizik 2020, *Marketing Science*](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mksc.2020.1226)).

Selected Recent Literature
-----

**LLMs as instruments for measuring consumer & brand perception**

- Li, Castelo, Katona & Sarvary (2024). [Determining the Validity of Large Language Models for Automated Perceptual Analysis](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mksc.2023.0454). *Marketing Science*, 43(2).
- Brand, Israeli & Ngwe (2024). [Using LLMs for Market Research](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4395751). HBS Working Paper 23-062.
- Arora, Chakraborty & Nishimura (2025). [AI–Human Hybrids for Marketing Research: Leveraging LLMs as Collaborators](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00222429241276529). *Journal of Marketing*, 89(2).
- Goli & Singh (2024). [Can Large Language Models Capture Human Preferences?](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mksc.2023.0306) *Marketing Science*, 43(4).
- Gui & Toubia (2025). [The Challenge of Using LLMs to Simulate Human Behavior: A Causal Inference Perspective](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.15524). Working paper.
- Toubia, Gui, Peng, Merlau, Li & Chen (2025). [Twin-2K-500: A Dataset for Building Digital Twins](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17479). Working paper + public dataset.
- Stromberg, Moe, Reutterer & Schweidel (2025). [Blind Spots in Broad Strokes: Caveats for the Use of LLMs in Marketing Research](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5145114). Working paper.

**Measuring the consumer voice at scale (text & images)**

- Liu, Dzyabura & Mizik (2020). [Visual Listening In: Extracting Brand Image Portrayed on Social Media](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mksc.2020.1226). *Marketing Science*, 39(4).
- Hartmann, Heitmann, Siebert & Schamp (2023). [More than a Feeling: Accuracy and Application of Sentiment Analysis](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167811622000477). *IJRM*, 40(1).
- Krugmann & Hartmann (2024). [Sentiment Analysis in the Age of Generative AI](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40547-024-00143-4). *Customer Needs and Solutions*, 11.
- Chakraborty, Kim & Sudhir (2022). [Attribute Sentiment Scoring with Online Text Reviews](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/00222437211052500). *JMR*, 59(3).
- Ringel (2023). [Multimarket Membership Mapping](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00222437221110460). *JMR*, 60(2).

**AI-generated content & AI influencers**

- Hartmann, Exner & Domdey (2025). [The Power of Generative Marketing: Can Generative AI Create Superhuman Visual Marketing Content?](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167811624000843) *IJRM*, 42(1).
- Zhang & Gosline (2023). [Human Favoritism, Not AI Aversion](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/judgment-and-decision-making/article/human-favoritism-not-ai-aversion-peoples-perceptions-and-bias-toward-generative-ai-human-experts-and-humangai-collaboration-in-persuasive-content-generation/419C4BD9CE82673EAF1D8F6C350C4FA8). *Judgment and Decision Making*, 18.
- Cascio Rizzo, Berger & Villarroel Ordenes (2023). [What Drives Virtual Influencer's Impact?](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4329150) Working paper.
- Matz, Teeny, Vaid, Peters, Harari & Cerf (2024). [The Potential of Generative AI for Personalized Persuasion at Scale](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-53755-0). *Scientific Reports*, 14.
- Ye, Yoganarasimhan & Zheng (2025). [LOLA: LLM-Assisted Online Learning Algorithm for Content Experiments](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mksc.2024.0990). *Marketing Science*.
